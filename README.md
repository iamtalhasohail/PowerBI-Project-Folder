# Data Jobs Dashboard with Power Bi
![Dashboard Page 1](/Images/Project%201%20Page%201.png)
## Introduction

Finding reliable information about the data job market is not easy. Salary figures, hiring trends, and job requirements are spread across many websites. This Power BI project brings it all into one *interactive report built on roughly 479K data job postings from 2024*. Whether you are applying for your **first data role, moving into data from another field, or considering a change**, you can see how many jobs are out there, what they pay, where they are located, and how each role compares to the others. 

## Skills Showcased

- 🧮 **Measures & KPIs:** Created measures for the key numbers, such as median yearly salary, median hourly salary, and total job count.
- 📊 **Core Charts:** Used **Column, Line, Area and Bar charts** to track job postings through 2024 and rank job titles by pay, plus a **Scatter Plot** to compare hourly and yearly salaries.
- 🥧 **Proportion Visuals:** Used **donut charts and a treemap** to break down remote work, insurance, degree requirements, and job types.
- 🎯 **KPI Cards, Gauges & Tables:** Highlighted headline metrics with **cards and gauges**, and added a table with sparklines for detailed comparisons.
- 🌍 **Geographic Analysis:** Mapped where data jobs are located around the world. Used **Map Charts** for this.
- 🎨 **Dashboard Design:** Arranged a clean, consistent layout across two pages so the story is easy to follow.
- 🖱️ **Interactive Reporting:**
  - **Slicers:** Filter the whole report by job title.
  - **Drill-through:** Jump from the overview to a detailed page for one job title.
  - **Buttons:** Navigate between pages, including a back button.

## Dashboard Overview 

### Page 1: Market Overview

![Dashboard Page 1](/Images/Project%201%20Page%201.png)

The first page gives a quick snapshot of the data job market in 2024. The cards at the top show the total number of postings along with the median yearly and hourly salary. Below them, you can see how hiring changed month by month, how hourly and yearly pay relate to each other, which job titles pay the most, and a table that compares each role side by side. Use the job title slicer to focus on one role.

### Page 2: Job Title Drill Through

![Dashboard Page 1](/Images/Project%201%20Page%202.png)

Pick a job title on the first page and click the drill-through button to open a detailed view of that role. This page shows its salary range, how often it offers remote work, health insurance, or no degree requirement, the platforms that post the most openings, the types of employment offered, and where the jobs are located around the world.

## Conclusion

Building this dashboard taught me how to go from raw data to a clear, interactive report. I learned how to choose the right chart for each question, use slicers and drill-through to let viewers explore on their own, and design a layout that stays readable. The result is a tool that helps job seekers compare data roles and make better-informed career decisions.
